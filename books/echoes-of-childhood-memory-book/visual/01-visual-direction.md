# Visual Direction — Version One

## Format recommendation

**Primary trim:** 6 × 9 in, portrait.  
**Digital master:** matching 2:3 portrait geometry.  
**Binding:** hardcover, matte finish.  
**Target extent:** 48–64 pages during design exploration; final print extent must satisfy the selected printer's binding minimum.

The supplied cover is 1024 × 1535 px, effectively a 2:3 composition. A 6 × 9 trim honours the design without cropping or artificial extension.

## Print-production fork

Two valid physical editions should be prototyped:

1. **Collector colour edition:** premium colour interior on white matte paper, preserving warm photography and found-object colour.
2. **Literary cream edition:** black-and-white or duotone interior on cream paper, using tonal illustrations and restrained artefacts.

For KDP hardcover, colour interiors require white paper; cream paper is available with black ink. Therefore “full-colour interior” and “cream paper” cannot be treated as the same KDP specification. A specialist short-run printer may offer additional combinations.

## Visual signature

The reader should recognise a page by five recurring features:

- warm ivory field rather than pure white;
- deep midnight-navy typography;
- restrained sepia/gold accents;
- full-page watercolour chapter openings contrasted with quiet text pages;
- found objects entering from margins as though resting between pages.

## Typography

### Display serif

Use an elegant high-contrast serif that matches the cover's literary character. Preferred accessible candidates for prototyping: Cormorant Garamond or EB Garamond. Final selection must be checked for licensing and print embedding.

### Body serif

Use a highly readable old-style serif: EB Garamond, Source Serif 4, or Libre Baskerville. Target 10.5–11.5 pt with generous leading, tested at physical size.

### Handwritten accent

Use sparingly for object annotations, dates, or the cover line only. Never use for body copy or long quotations.

### Small capitals / tracking

Chapter metadata, “MEMORY FRAGMENT”, and listening cues may use tracked small capitals in navy or faded gold.

## Page architecture

- Outer margins should feel generous; inner margins must anticipate hardcover binding.
- Chapter opening images may bleed fully.
- Chapter title pages should contain no running header and no visible folio.
- Text pages should average 45–60 characters per line.
- Memory Fragments should occupy their own quiet block, never a decorative quotation box.
- Last lines may be isolated only when emotionally earned.
- Objects should appear irregularly, not on every spread.

## Cover assessment

The supplied cover is approved as the visual anchor. Its strengths are:

- immediate “memory box” concept;
- restrained human presence through the Polaroid;
- strong hierarchy and readable title;
- coherent navy, parchment, and warm-brown palette;
- objects spanning different forms of childhood memory.

Production limitations:

- the current 1024 × 1535 source is suitable for design development and digital preview, but not yet sufficient as a final 300 DPI 6 × 9 front cover;
- a 6 × 9 front at 300 DPI requires at least 1800 × 2700 px before bleed;
- a printed hardcover wrap cannot be finalised until page count, paper, binding, spine width, and printer template are locked;
- the front design must later be extended into spine and back cover rather than merely enlarged.

## Music integration

The first premium eBook edition contains no QR codes and no music links. Permanent track links may be considered in a later edition after the author supplies them.

The present edition must remain complete and coherent as a standalone book.

## Interior image policy

The supplied two-part overview boards are reference sheets, not finished spreads. Their twelve visual ideas have been recreated as individual 2:3 portrait illustrations in a consistent watercolour-and-gouache language. Each illustration opens its corresponding chapter without embedded titles, captions, logos, or numbering. The overview boards do not appear in the finished eBook.
