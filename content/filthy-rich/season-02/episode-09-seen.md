FILTHY RICH

Season Two — The Relationship

Episode 09 — Seen

Emily noticed the silence before she noticed the breakup.

It arrived in small pieces.

Replies became reactions.

Phone calls became voice notes.

Voice notes became:

Sorry, busy today.

Busy stayed for three weeks.

One Friday evening, she asked Daniel the question both of them had been avoiding.

“Are we okay?”

Daniel looked at the table.

It was easier than looking at her.

“I don’t know.”

Emily nodded.

She had known that answer for a while.

The conversation lasted forty-eight minutes.

Nobody shouted.

Nobody left dramatically.

Nobody accused anyone of betrayal.

They simply reached the end of something that had been trying very hard not to end.

When Daniel stood to leave, Emily hugged him.

It felt familiar.

Which somehow made it worse.

At the door, Daniel smiled.

“I really hope we can stay friends.”

Emily smiled too.

People often smiled while handing each other impossible jobs.

The following morning, Emily opened Instagram.

Their photographs remained.

The beach.

Paris.

Christmas.

The wedding they had attended.

The badly cooked barbecue.

The birthday where Daniel wore the ridiculous hat.

A stranger would have called it a beautiful relationship.

Emily began deleting.

Not because she hated the memories.

Because she no longer knew who they belonged to.

She stopped after the third photograph.

Deleting seven years one square at a time felt administrative.

Instead, she archived them.

The profile became quieter.

Not emptier.

Just quieter.

On Monday, Chloe called.

“So, is it official?”

“I think so.”

“You think?”

“We didn’t really use that word.”

“What did you use?”

Emily thought.

Words like:

Space.

Timing.

Different directions.

Nobody had used the word over.

That evening, Daniel posted a photograph of a sunset.

The caption read:

Some seasons teach more than others.

Emily looked at it.

Three hundred people liked it.

Several commented:

Proud of you, brother.

She wondered what exactly they believed had happened.

The next weekend, Emily met her friend Ava for lunch.

“How are you?”

Emily stirred her coffee.

“I answer that differently depending on who’s asking.”

Ava smiled.

“How are you when I’m asking?”

Emily thought for a moment.

“Lonely.”

Ava reached across the table.

Emily didn’t move her hand.

She simply looked at it.

It had been a long time since someone had touched her without needing reassurance.

A week later, Daniel messaged.

Hope you’re doing okay.

Emily replied:

I am. Hope you are too.

Neither of them was lying.

Neither of them was telling the whole story.

Months passed.

The algorithm refused to cooperate.

Emily continued seeing memories.

Suggested reels.

Old tagged photographs.

People asking:

Where’s Daniel?

She answered politely.

Sometimes with words.

Sometimes by pretending not to see the message.

One evening, she opened her profile.

The photographs looked balanced again.

Travel.

Friends.

Books.

Coffee.

A dog that wasn’t hers.

The relationship had disappeared carefully.

As though it had never interrupted the grid.

She closed the app.

Then opened her photo gallery.

Thousands of pictures.

Daniel laughing in the supermarket.

Daniel asleep on the train.

Daniel dancing badly in the kitchen.

None of them had ever been posted.

She selected one.

Daniel, covered in flour, trying to make pizza.

She almost sent it to him.

Then she did.

The photograph appeared beneath their last exchange.

Hope you’re doing okay.

I am. Hope you are too.

Emily waited.

Daniel began typing.

Then stopped.

Seen 10:43 p.m.
