FILTHY RICH

Season Two — The Relationship

Episode 03 — Anniversary Post

Lena and Michael had been together for seven years.

Online, they had been happy for all of them.

Every anniversary arrived with a photograph and a caption long enough to suggest gratitude had required editing.

This year, Lena started writing the post four days early.

She opened with:

Seven years with my best friend.

Then deleted best friend.

Michael had not felt like her best friend since February.

She tried:

Seven years of choosing each other.

That was better.

Choosing sounded active.

It also sounded like they had considered alternatives.

She deleted it.

Michael found her on the sofa with her phone in one hand and an old photograph open on the screen.

“What are you doing?”

“Nothing.”

“You’ve been staring at our wedding for twenty minutes.”

“It’s our anniversary tomorrow.”

“I know.”

“Did you plan anything?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

“Dinner.”

“Where?”

“The Italian place.”

Lena looked at him.

“The normal one?”

Michael paused.

“The one we like.”

“That’s not what I asked.”

He sat beside her.

“What did you want?”

“I don’t know.”

“You usually do.”

She locked the phone.

“I just thought seven years might feel more… intentional.”

Michael looked at the framed photographs on the shelf.

The wedding.

Paris.

The beach.

The restaurant where they had celebrated five years.

Everything important had eventually become rectangular.

“I booked the place we always say we should go back to,” he said.

“That’s nice.”

“It didn’t sound nice when you said it.”

“I said it was nice.”

“You said it like a nurse.”

Lena stood.

“I’m going to bed.”

“Are we fighting?”

“No.”

They had developed several ways to say yes.

The next morning, Michael placed coffee beside her.

“Happy anniversary.”

“Happy anniversary.”

He kissed her forehead.

Lena opened Instagram.

Three years ago, her caption had said:

Still laughing. Still growing. Still us.

Last year:

Home is wherever you are.

She had written that from the guest room.

Michael sat across from her.

“You’re doing the post, aren’t you?”

“I always do the post.”

“You don’t have to.”

“I know.”

She continued scrolling.

Her friend Sophie had posted flowers.

Chloe had posted a hotel room.

A former colleague had posted:

Ten years. Ten lessons. One great love.

Lena saved it.

Not because she liked it.

Because it had structure.

At lunch, Michael sent her a message.

Looking forward to tonight.

She reacted with a heart.

Then stared at the heart.

It looked warmer than she felt.

At seven, they arrived at the restaurant.

The table was near the kitchen.

Lena noticed immediately.

Michael did not.

He had ordered her favorite wine.

She photographed the bottle before it was opened.

“Can you move your hand closer?” she asked.

“To the wine?”

“To mine.”

Michael placed his hand beside hers.

Lena took the photograph.

Their wedding rings caught the light.

Perfect.

Michael looked at the screen.

“That’s a good one.”

“It’s okay.”

“It looks like us.”

Lena zoomed in.

“It looks better than us.”

Michael withdrew his hand.

The waiter arrived.

“Celebrating something?”

“Seven years,” Lena said.

“Congratulations.”

“Thank you.”

Michael smiled.

Lena smiled too.

The waiter left.

They stopped.

The food came quickly.

Michael told a story about work.

Lena listened to half of it.

The other half became caption ideas.

Seven years of love, laughter and learning.

Too generic.

Not perfect. Just real.

Too honest.

Seven years, and I’d still choose you.

She looked at Michael.

He was cutting bread.

He looked tired.

Older than in the photograph she planned to use.

So did she.

“Can I ask you something?” Lena said.

“Sure.”

“Are we okay?”

Michael stopped cutting.

“That’s a big question for pasta.”

“I’m serious.”

“I know.”

He placed the knife down.

“I think we’re tired.”

“That’s not the same as okay.”

“No.”

“Do you still want this?”

Michael looked around the restaurant.

The kitchen door opened behind her.

Someone dropped a tray.

A baby cried two tables away.

Nothing waited politely for their marriage to become clear.

“Yes,” he said.

Lena watched him.

“That took a long time.”

“I wanted to answer the right question.”

“What question did you think I asked?”

“Whether I still want us.”

“And?”

“Yes.”

He leaned back.

“But not like this.”

“What does that mean?”

Michael looked at her phone.

Lena turned it face down.

The anniversary post was still open.

Seven years with—

Nothing after it.

Michael reached across the table.

This time, she did not photograph his hand.

They talked until the restaurant began stacking chairs.

Not beautifully.

Not efficiently.

They repeated themselves.

They misunderstood each other.

Michael apologized badly.

Lena apologized more clearly, then became annoyed that his apology had been worse.

At one point, they laughed.

Not because the conversation was fixed.

Because Michael had spilled wine on the tablecloth while trying to explain emotional distance.

By the time they left, the photograph of their hands had become less important.

At home, Lena opened Instagram again.

The caption box waited.

Michael stood behind her.

“What are you writing?”

“I don’t know.”

She typed:

Seven years. Still learning how to be honest before the caption.

Michael read it.

“That sounds like we’re in trouble.”

“We are in trouble.”

“Do people need to know?”

Lena looked at the photograph.

The rings.

The wine.

The perfect distance between their hands.

“No.”

She deleted the caption.

Then posted the photograph with:

Seven years. ❤️

The comments arrived immediately.

Couple goals.

Forever looks good on you.

The kind of love we all want.

Lena read each one.

Michael went to bed.

At midnight, Lena opened a note called Anniversary.

She pasted the longer caption at the top.

Six older drafts moved down.

None had ever been posted.
