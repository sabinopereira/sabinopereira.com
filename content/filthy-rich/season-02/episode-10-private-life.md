FILTHY RICH

Season Two — The Relationship

Episode 10 — Private Life

Nathan and Olivia had become experts at protecting their relationship.

Mostly from each other.

Online, they shared very little.

No anniversary posts.

No matching outfits.

No long captions about soulmates.

People admired them for it.

“So private,” friends would say.

“So mature.”

Nathan liked hearing that.

Privacy sounded healthier than silence.

Every Friday, they had dinner together.

It was a rule.

Not a ritual.

A rule.

Rules survived longer.

On this particular Friday, they sat across from each other in the same restaurant they had visited for years.

The waiter already knew their order.

Neither noticed.

Nathan looked at the menu.

Olivia looked out of the window.

“How was your week?” he asked.

“Busy.”

“You?”

“The same.”

The waiter returned with two glasses of water.

“Anything to celebrate tonight?”

Nathan smiled politely.

“Just Friday.”

The waiter nodded.

He had served them often enough to know that Friday was carrying more weight than it admitted.

The food arrived.

They thanked the waiter.

They complimented the food.

They discussed a leaking tap.

A supermarket promotion.

Whether the car needed servicing.

Nothing was wrong.

Nothing was particularly alive either.

Halfway through dinner, a young couple sat at the next table.

They laughed too loudly.

Took photographs of everything.

Argued playfully about which dessert looked better on camera.

Olivia watched them.

Nathan noticed.

“They remind me of us.”

Olivia smiled.

“No.”

“No?”

“We were never that loud.”

Nathan laughed.

“That’s true.”

She looked back at the couple.

“They still seem curious about each other.”

Nathan lowered his fork.

The sentence landed quietly.

“What are you curious about?” he asked.

Olivia looked at him.

The question felt unfamiliar.

She searched for an answer.

“I don’t know.”

Nathan nodded.

After dinner, they walked home.

Two people who had become extremely efficient at sharing a life.

At the apartment, Nathan made tea.

Olivia sat on the sofa.

The television remained off.

“You know,” Nathan said.

“What?”

“I don’t actually know what you’ve been thinking about lately.”

Olivia smiled faintly.

“I don’t think you’ve asked.”

“I thought you’d tell me.”

“I thought you’d ask.”

Silence.

Not hostile.

Simply unused.

Nathan handed her the tea.

“When did we stop talking?”

Olivia looked into the cup.

“I don’t think we stopped.”

“What then?”

“We became very good at updating each other.”

Nathan sat beside her.

Work.

Bills.

Appointments.

Shopping lists.

Holiday plans.

Weather.

Everything except themselves.

He reached for the television remote.

Then placed it back on the table.

“So.”

“So.”

“What are you thinking about?”

Olivia smiled.

“You asked.”

“I know.”

She took a long breath.

Then she answered.

Not in one sentence.

Not beautifully.

Not efficiently.

They talked until the tea became cold.

Then made another.

They interrupted each other.

They misunderstood each other twice.

Nathan apologised for something that had happened three years earlier.

Olivia admitted she had almost stopped expecting questions.

Nothing was solved.

Near midnight, Nathan looked at his phone.

There were no notifications.

No photographs.

No proof they had spent the evening together.

He slipped the phone back into his pocket.

“Should we have taken a picture?” he asked.

Olivia laughed.

“For who?”

Nathan looked around the quiet apartment.

The cups.

The blanket.

The conversation waiting for tomorrow.

“No one,” he said.

The next morning, Olivia’s phone vibrated.

Chloe had sent a message.

How was your peaceful Friday?

Olivia began typing:

We realised we haven’t really spoken in months.

She looked at the sentence.

Then deleted it.

Really good.

Chloe replied with a white heart.

Nathan entered carrying two coffees.

“Where were we?”

Olivia turned the phone face down.
