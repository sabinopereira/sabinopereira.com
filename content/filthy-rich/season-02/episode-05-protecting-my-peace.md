FILTHY RICH

Season Two — The Relationship

Episode 05 — Protecting My Peace

Nina blocked people carefully.

Never in anger.

That would have been reactive.

She blocked them after reflection.

Usually within three minutes.

Her ex-boyfriend had been blocked on six platforms.

Her cousin had been blocked after a disagreement about Christmas.

Her former best friend had been blocked for “bringing strange energy.”

The woman from the gym had not been blocked yet.

She was muted.

Nina believed in escalation procedures.

She had once spent two years being told she remembered every argument incorrectly.

The notes began as evidence.

Eventually, everything became evidence.

Her current boyfriend, Adam, knew the language.

When Nina said she needed space, he gave her space.

When she said she was setting a boundary, he stopped asking questions.

When she said she was protecting her peace, he understood that peace currently meant not hearing his version.

They had been together for eight months.

Seven of them had been described online as healthy.

On Thursday evening, Adam arrived twenty minutes late for dinner.

Nina was already seated.

Her phone rested beside the untouched water.

“You’re late,” she said.

“I know. I’m sorry.”

“You didn’t communicate.”

“I texted.”

“At 7:12.”

“I was meant to be here at seven.”

“Exactly.”

Adam sat down.

“There was traffic.”

Nina looked at him.

“Traffic is not communication.”

“No.”

“It’s information.”

“Okay.”

The waiter approached.

“Would you like a few more minutes?”

Nina smiled.

“We’re fine.”

Adam looked at the menu.

The waiter left.

Nina turned her phone face down.

“I just need you to understand that this isn’t about twenty minutes.”

“What is it about?”

“Consistency.”

Adam nodded.

“I understand.”

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

“What do you understand?”

Adam looked toward the bar.

“That being late made you feel unimportant.”

Nina paused.

“That’s not what I said.”

“No.”

“But it’s interesting that’s where your mind went.”

Adam took a breath.

“I’m trying.”

“I shouldn’t have to teach someone how to value me.”

He nodded again.

The waiter returned.

Nina ordered grilled salmon.

Adam ordered pasta.

“Actually,” Nina said, “we’re sharing.”

Adam looked up.

“We are?”

“I thought we were.”

“We can.”

The waiter wrote it down.

Nina smiled.

“Thank you.”

The smile remained until he walked away.

Adam lowered his voice.

“Can we just have dinner?”

“That’s what I’m trying to do.”

“No. We’re reviewing dinner.”

Nina sat back.

“I’m not going to engage if you’re becoming defensive.”

“I’m not defensive.”

“You’re raising your voice.”

“I’m whispering.”

Nina looked at him.

Adam stopped.

Adam looked at the candle between them.

It had begun to lean.

Nina picked up her phone.

“What are you doing?”

“Nothing.”

“You’re typing.”

“I’m making a note.”

“About me?”

“About how I feel.”

Adam leaned back.

Nina opened her Notes app.

She had a folder called Patterns.

Inside were dates.

Times.

Phrases.

Screenshots.

Three voice notes she had recorded after arguments so she would not later minimise what had happened.

She typed:

Dismissed conversation by asking to “just have dinner.”

Then locked the phone.

The salmon arrived first.

Nina photographed it.

Adam watched.

“Seriously?”

“What?”

“You’re posting dinner?”

“I’m not posting.”

She adjusted the brightness.

“You’re editing dinner.”

“I like the lighting.”

The pasta arrived.

Nina moved both plates closer together.

Adam placed his hand on the table.

She took the photograph before he could move it.

Two plates.

One candle.

His hand.

No traffic.

No folder called Patterns.

She posted it with:

Date nights matter. ❤️

Adam stared at the screen.

“You just told me we’re not okay.”

“We’re having dinner.”

“That’s not the same thing.”

“I don’t owe people our private moments.”

“But you owe them a fake public one?”

Nina put the phone down.

“I’m not doing this here.”

“We are already doing this here.”

She became very still.

“I need to protect my peace.”

Adam looked around the restaurant.

The music was soft.

The lights were low.

Nobody nearby was speaking loudly.

“From me?”

“From this energy.”

“What energy?”

“This.”

He waited.

Nina did not define it.

Some words became less useful when made specific.

The rest of dinner passed politely.

They divided the food.

They discussed work.

They smiled at the waiter.

Adam paid.

Outside, Nina stopped near the entrance.

“There’s something I need to say.”

Adam placed his hands in his pockets.

“I don’t think this is healthy for me.”

“What isn’t?”

“The dynamic.”

“What dynamic?”

“The fact that I have to keep explaining why basic consideration matters.”

Adam looked at her.

“I was twenty minutes late.”

“It isn’t about twenty minutes.”

“I know.”

“Then why do you keep saying that?”

“Because that’s what happened.”

Nina shook her head.

“That’s your version.”

“It’s the clock’s version.”

She looked away.

Cars passed behind him.

A couple exited the restaurant laughing.

Nina crossed her arms.

“I think we should take a step back.”

Adam nodded slowly.

“How far?”

“I don’t know.”

“Should I call you tomorrow?”

“No.”

“Next week?”

“I need you to respect the boundary without negotiating it.”

“All right.”

He began walking toward his car.

Nina watched him leave.

She had expected more resistance.

Resistance would have confirmed the pattern.

At home, she opened Instagram.

The dinner photograph had performed well.

Her friend Chloe had commented:

Healthy love looks good on you.

Nina stared at the words.

Then replied with a white heart.

Adam did not message the next day.

Or the day after.

On Sunday, Nina posted a black screen with white text.

Protecting your peace will cost you people who benefited from your lack of boundaries.

Hundreds of people shared it.

Adam saw the post.

He did not respond.

For the first time in the relationship, he respected exactly what she had asked for.

Nina checked his profile twice that evening.

Then she blocked him.

Her phone remained silent.

Nina waited.
