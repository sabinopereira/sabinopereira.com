FILTHY RICH

Season Two — The Relationship

Episode 02 — Green Flags

Emma had a list.

Not on paper.

On her phone.

It was called Standards.

She updated it regularly.

Green flags.

Red flags.

Beige flags.

Things that demonstrated emotional intelligence.

Things that demonstrated unresolved childhood wounds.

Things that demonstrated secure attachment.

Things that demonstrated avoidant attachment pretending to be secure.

By thirty-two, Emma had become remarkably good at recognising patterns.

She had become considerably worse at recognising people.

On Thursday evening, she met Oliver for coffee.

He arrived three minutes early.

Green flag.

He held the café door open.

Green flag.

He remembered her favourite tea from their messages.

Green flag.

He listened without interrupting.

Green flag.

Emma smiled.

Oliver noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“What?”

“You’re just very consistent.”

“Thank you.”

She typed something into her phone.

“What was that?”

“A reminder.”

It wasn’t.

It was another green flag.

Oliver talked about his parents.

He admitted they argued often.

“They’ve been married forty years,” he said. “They’re still figuring each other out.”

Emma nodded politely.

The list remained silent.

“What about you?” Oliver asked.

“My parents divorced.”

“I’m sorry.”

“It happens.”

They talked for another hour.

Oliver laughed easily.

Asked questions.

Never checked his phone.

Walked her home.

When they reached her building, he smiled.

“I’d like to see you again.”

“I’d like that too.”

He left.

Emma stood in the hallway.

Then opened the list.

She added:

✓ Good listener

✓ Respectful

✓ Emotionally available

She paused.

Then wrote:

Mentioned parents arguing.

She stared at it.

Was that honesty?

Or unresolved family dynamics?

She wasn’t sure.

She moved it into Things to Observe.

The next morning, she sent the date summary to Chloe.

Green flags: 14

Possible concerns: 2

Chemistry: 7.5/10

Future potential: High

Chloe replied.

Did you like him?

Emma looked at the message.

Then at the list.

She hadn’t measured that.

The second date went well.

The third went better.

Oliver cooked dinner.

He burnt the garlic bread.

He laughed.

Emma laughed too.

Then wondered whether laughing at mistakes suggested emotional maturity or a lack of standards.

She reached for her phone.

Oliver began scraping the burnt edges with a knife.

“I’m sorry,” he said.

“It’s okay.”

“No, really.”

“Oliver.”

He looked at her.

“You don’t have to fix it.”

“I’m not fixing it.”

He held up the bread.

“I’m removing evidence.”

Emma smiled.

For a moment, she forgot what the smile demonstrated.

Then she added humour under pressure to the list.

By the end of the first month, Oliver had accumulated twenty-eight green flags.

He called when he said he would.

He spoke kindly about his former girlfriend.

He had close friends.

He knew how to apologise without explaining the apology.

When Emma said she needed space, he gave it to her.

When she asked why he had not called during that space, he looked confused.

Emma added respects boundaries.

At two months, she introduced him to Chloe.

Oliver arrived with wine.

He remembered Chloe’s name.

He asked about her work.

He did not try too hard.

Afterwards, Chloe sent a message.

He seems lovely.

Emma replied immediately.

I know.

She read the sentence twice.

It sounded almost disappointed.

Three months after their first coffee, Oliver had accumulated forty-seven green flags.

He was consistent.

Available.

Kind to waiters.

Close to his family without being dependent on them.

He communicated clearly.

He respected boundaries.

Emma had never dated anyone so suitable.

Chloe called on Sunday afternoon.

“How are things with Oliver?”

“Really good.”

“Still no red flags?”

“Nothing significant.”

“Do you love him?”

Emma looked towards the sofa.

Oliver was reading.

One sock was missing.

“We’re highly compatible.”

There was a pause.

“That wasn’t the question,” Chloe said.

After the call, Emma sat beside him.

His book fell to the floor.

He closed his eyes.

The kitchen was untidy.

The garlic bread had improved.

Emma opened the document called Standards.

She scrolled through the green flags.

Reliability.

Self-awareness.

Healthy conflict.

Shared values.

Oliver opened his eyes.

“Emma?”

“Yes?”

“I love you.”

She smiled.

It seemed like the emotionally available response.

“I love you too.”

Oliver closed his eyes.

Emma searched the document for the word love.

It did not appear once.
