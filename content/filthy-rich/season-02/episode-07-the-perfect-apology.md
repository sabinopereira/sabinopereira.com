FILTHY RICH

Season Two — The Relationship

Episode 07 — The Perfect Apology

Aaron had apologized before.

This time, he wanted to do it properly.

The mistake had been simple.

He had forgotten that Mia was presenting her first major project at work.

She had reminded him twice.

He had placed it in his calendar.

Then moved the reminder because it overlapped with lunch.

At 3:17 p.m., Mia sent him a message.

It went well.

Aaron looked at the screen.

Then at the calendar.

The presentation had started at two.

He replied immediately.

I knew you’d be amazing. ❤️

Mia read the message.

She did not answer.

At six, Aaron called.

“I’m sorry.”

“You forgot.”

“I didn’t forget.”

“You remembered after it happened.”

“That’s different.”

“It is.”

Aaron walked towards the kitchen.

“I had a crazy day.”

“So did I.”

“I know.”

“No, you don’t.”

He opened the refrigerator.

There was nothing inside that could help.

“I’m really proud of you.”

“You weren’t there.”

“You said partners weren’t allowed.”

“That isn’t what I meant.”

Aaron knew this.

Knowing it now was not useful.

“I want to make it up to you.”

Mia became quiet.

The sentence had arrived before.

Usually carrying flowers.

“I don’t need you to make it up to me.”

“What do you need?”

“I needed you to remember.”

Aaron leaned against the counter.

“I said I’m sorry.”

“I know.”

“What else do you want me to say?”

Mia looked at the photograph her coworker had taken after the presentation.

She was smiling.

Her hands were shaking.

Aaron had not seen either.

“I don’t want another sentence,” she said.

The call ended.

Aaron stood alone in the kitchen.

He understood the problem.

Mia did not feel seen.

This could be addressed.

He opened Instagram.

A couple he followed had recently posted a public apology.

Black-and-white photograph.

Long caption.

Thousands of comments praising accountability.

Aaron read it twice.

The structure was excellent.

He opened his Notes app.

He typed:

Sometimes loving someone means admitting when you fall short.

Good opening.

He continued.

Today, I failed to show up for the person who has always shown up for me.

That was not entirely true.

Mia had missed his office party in March.

But this was not the time for balance.

He wrote:

No excuses. No defensiveness. Just growth.

He deleted defensiveness.

It sounded defensive.

He tried:

I’m learning that intention means nothing without action.

Aaron sat back.

That was strong.

He added a photograph of himself and Mia from a wedding the previous summer.

They looked elegant.

Happy.

Accountable in advance.

Before posting, he sent Mia a screenshot.

Thinking of sharing this.

Mia called immediately.

“You’re posting an apology?”

“I wanted to acknowledge what happened.”

“To who?”

“To you.”

“Then why does everyone else need to see it?”

Aaron looked at the caption.

“It’s not for everyone else.”

“It’s on Instagram.”

“I’m taking responsibility.”

Mia laughed once.

“Aaron, you forgot something important to me.”

“I know.”

“And now you want people to watch you understand that.”

“That’s not what this is.”

“What is it?”

Aaron looked again at the photograph.

They looked very happy.

“It’s about showing that I’m serious.”

“Show me.”

He became quiet.

Mia continued.

“Do you know what would make me feel better?”

“What?”

“Ask me how it went.”

Aaron closed the Notes app.

“How did it go?”

Mia waited.

He sounded sincere.

Late.

But sincere.

She told him about the room.

The questions.

The moment the projector stopped working.

The joke she made.

The applause.

The promotion her manager hinted at afterwards.

Aaron listened.

He almost interrupted twice.

He did not.

When she finished, he said:

“I’m sorry I missed that.”

This apology was shorter.

It performed badly.

Mia believed it.

They spoke for another twenty minutes.

At the end of the call, Aaron deleted the Instagram draft.

Then restored it from Recently Deleted.

Not to post.

Just in case he needed the wording later.

The following morning, he sent Mia breakfast.

No caption.

No flowers.

No public statement.

Just coffee and the pastry she liked.

She sent back:

Thank you.

Aaron stared at the message.

No heart.

No photograph.

No audience.

Two days later, Aaron posted a picture of them at dinner.

The caption read:

Learning. Growing. Grateful.

Mia saw it.

She looked across the table.

“You couldn’t help yourself.”

Aaron smiled.

“I kept it short.”

Her phone vibrated.

Aaron invited you to collaborate on a post.

Mia selected:

Not now.
