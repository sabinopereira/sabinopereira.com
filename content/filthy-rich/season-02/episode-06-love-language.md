FILTHY RICH

Season Two — The Relationship

Episode 06 — Love Language

Ben’s love language was gifts.

He had learned this after forgetting three birthdays.

Before that, his love language had been apologizing.

Before that, work.

His girlfriend, Rachel, preferred quality time.

Ben respected this.

He bought her a watch.

“It’s beautiful,” Rachel said.

“I knew you’d like it.”

She turned it over in her hand.

The face was small.

Gold.

Quietly expensive.

“Why a watch?”

Ben smiled.

“So you know I’m always thinking about our time together.”

Rachel looked at him.

“That’s almost funny.”

He kissed her forehead.

The watch had been easier than Saturday.

Saturday required availability.

Ben’s calendar did not contain availability.

It contained meetings, calls, follow-ups and several blocks marked FOCUS, during which people still called him.

Rachel had asked for dinner twice that month.

Ben had sent flowers both times.

The first bouquet said:

Thinking of you.

The second said:

Soon.

Soon had become one of Ben’s more reliable commitments.

On Wednesday, Rachel called while he was leaving the office.

“Are you coming over?”

“I’m trying.”

“That isn’t an answer.”

“It’s the most honest one I have.”

She was quiet.

Ben continued walking.

“I have one more call.”

“You always have one more call.”

“This one matters.”

“They all matter.”

“Exactly.”

Rachel exhaled.

“Do I?”

Ben stopped beside his car.

“Of course you do.”

“When?”

He opened the door.

“That’s not fair.”

“It was a question.”

“I’m under a lot of pressure right now.”

“I know.”

“This quarter is important.”

“So was last quarter.”

Ben sat in the car.

He did not start it.

“I’m doing this for us.”

Rachel had heard this before.

The sentence had excellent benefits and no delivery date.

“What does ‘for us’ mean?” she asked.

“Security. Options. A future.”

“I asked you to have dinner.”

Ben looked at the dashboard clock.

7:42.

The restaurant reservation had been for seven.

“I can still come.”

“You said that an hour ago.”

“I’m saying it again.”

“That doesn’t make it newer.”

Ben closed his eyes.

“I don’t know what you want me to say.”

“I want you to stop saying things and arrive.”

The call ended.

Ben sat in the car for another minute.

Then opened a shopping app.

Rachel had saved a handbag two weeks earlier.

He remembered because the link was still in their messages.

The bag cost more than the dinner.

It was available for same-day delivery.

Ben selected gift wrapping.

Under Add a message, he typed:

For everything I don’t say enough.

He deleted it.

Too revealing.

He tried:

You deserve the world.

Better.

The world could be delivered between nine and eleven.

The next morning, Rachel sent a photograph of the bag.

Thank you. It’s lovely.

Ben smiled.

He reacted with a heart.

Then returned to the meeting.

That evening, Rachel carried the bag to dinner with her sister.

“It’s gorgeous,” her sister said.

Rachel placed it on the empty chair beside her.

“He missed dinner again.”

Her sister looked at the bag.

“At least he has taste.”

Rachel laughed.

Not because it was funny.

The waiter brought two menus.

Then a third by mistake.

Rachel looked at it for a moment before handing it back.

On Friday, Ben arrived at her apartment with a small velvet box.

Rachel opened the door.

“What’s that?”

“Nothing dramatic.”

“That usually means dramatic.”

He smiled.

Inside was a bracelet.

Thin gold.

A small charm shaped like a compass.

Rachel held it carefully.

“Why a compass?”

“So you always know where home is.”

She looked at him.

“You haven’t been here in six days.”

Ben’s smile changed shape.

“I’m here now.”

“For how long?”

He checked his phone.

Rachel noticed.

Ben turned it over.

“Tonight.”

“Until?”

“Rachel.”

“Until?”

He looked toward the kitchen.

Her table was set for two.

No flowers.

No gift wrap.

Just food.

“I have an early morning.”

She nodded.

“Of course.”

They sat down.

Ben complimented the meal.

Rachel asked about work.

He answered fully.

When he asked about her week, his phone vibrated.

He ignored it.

Then looked.

Then ignored it again.

Rachel watched the entire negotiation.

“You can answer.”

“I’m here.”

“Your body is.”

Ben placed the phone in his jacket pocket.

“There.”

Rachel picked up her glass.

“Do you know what my love language is?”

“Quality time.”

“You remember.”

“Of course.”

“Then why do you keep speaking to me in jewelry?”

Ben leaned back.

“I’m trying to show you I care.”

“I know.”

“Then what’s the problem?”

Rachel touched the compass charm.

“It keeps pointing to places you aren’t.”

Ben did not answer.

The food cooled between them.

His phone vibrated again.

This time, neither looked at it.

After dinner, Ben helped clear the plates.

He stayed until ten fifteen.

Rachel did not ask him to stay longer.

At the door, he kissed her.

“I love you.”

“I know.”

He smiled.

“That sounded ominous.”

“It wasn’t meant to.”

He left.

Rachel stood in the hallway.

The watch on her wrist said 10:18.

The bracelet pointed nowhere useful.

The handbag sat beside the door.

Together, they had cost more than a weekend away.

Ben drove home feeling that the evening had gone badly.

At home, he opened the shopping app.

Their anniversary was in three weeks.

He searched:

Meaningful gifts for her.

The first result was a weekend retreat.

Give the gift of time.

Ben selected the most expensive package.

Under Choose dates, he selected:

Decide later.
