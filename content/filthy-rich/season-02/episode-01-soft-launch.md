FILTHY RICH

Season Two — The Relationship

Episode 01 — Soft Launch

Sophie had been dating Ryan for eleven weeks.

Online, he existed in parts.

A hand holding coffee.

A shoulder beside her at dinner.

Two shoes beneath a restaurant table.

The back of his head at sunset.

Never the face.

The face suggested commitment.

The shoulder suggested possibility.

Sophie called it privacy.

Her friends called it marketing.

On Friday evening, Ryan arrived at her apartment carrying flowers.

“They’re beautiful,” Sophie said.

She photographed them before putting them in water.

“Should I hold them?” Ryan asked.

“No.”

“Okay.”

“Actually, hold them.”

Ryan picked them up.

“Like this?”

“Lower.”

He lowered them.

“Too low.”

He raised them.

“Now it looks like they’re yours.”

“They are mine.”

“You bought them for me.”

“Right.”

Sophie took the photograph without his face.

Perfect.

She posted it with no caption.

Just a white heart.

Within three minutes, her friends began messaging.

WHO IS HE?

Finally.

Is this the gym guy?

Hard launch immediately.

Sophie showed Ryan the screen.

“They’re excited.”

“About the flowers?”

“About us.”

Ryan looked at the photograph.

There was no us.

Only his wrist.

“Do they know it’s me?”

“Not yet.”

“Why not?”

“I don’t want people in our business.”

Ryan looked at the twenty-seven replies.

“Your business appears busy.”

At dinner, Sophie chose a restaurant with mirrored walls.

Mirrors created accidental intimacy.

Ryan opened the menu.

Sophie opened the camera.

“Can you move your hand closer?”

“To what?”

“My glass.”

“Why?”

“So people know I’m not alone.”

“They don’t know who I am.”

“That’s the point.”

Ryan moved his hand.

Sophie took the photograph.

His watch was visible.

His face was not.

“That watch is scratched,” she said.

“It tells time.”

“It also tells struggle.”

She adjusted the angle.

The scratch disappeared.

Ryan ordered pasta.

Sophie ordered fish.

The fish photographed better.

When the food arrived, she moved both plates towards her side of the table.

“Do you want mine too?” Ryan asked.

“Just for a second.”

The second lasted four minutes.

By the time Ryan began eating, the pasta had stopped steaming.

Sophie posted the photograph.

Good food. Better company.

Ryan looked at the screen.

“Better company is my hand?”

“People know.”

“Know what?”

“That I’m happy.”

Ryan looked at the photograph again.

“You want them to know you’re with someone.”

He put down his fork.

“I’m just not sure you want them to know you’re with me.”

“That’s not fair.”

Sophie placed the phone face down.

It remained there until it vibrated.

Her friend Chloe had replied:

He has nice hands.

Sophie smiled.

Ryan waited.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

She turned the phone over and typed:

You should see the rest.

Then deleted it.

Too revealing.

She replaced it with:

Maybe one day.

At home, Ryan stood by the window while Sophie reviewed the evening.

Flowers.

Glasses.

Food.

His sleeve.

His shoes.

The shadow of his face on a wall.

She selected the best four.

Ryan watched.

“Why don’t you just post a picture of us?”

Sophie stopped scrolling.

“Because once you post someone, people expect updates.”

“So?”

“If anything changes, they notice.”

She had deleted a face before.

People had noticed the empty spaces.

“I’m here, Sophie,” Ryan said.

“I know.”

“Then why do I keep disappearing?”

Sophie locked the phone.

The following week, Ryan posted a photograph of her.

Her face.

Her name.

No mystery.

No careful angle.

The caption read:

Sunday.

Sophie saw it within thirty seconds.

She called immediately.

“You posted me.”

“Yes.”

“Without asking.”

“You were there.”

“That isn’t the same thing.”

“You look good.”

“That’s not the point.”

Sophie opened the photograph again.

She did look good.

Relaxed.

Happy.

Unprepared.

Three of Ryan’s friends had already liked it.

One had commented:

Finally met the famous Sophie.

She stared at the word famous.

Not because she was known.

Because she had been discussed.

Ryan’s life contained a version of her she did not control.

“Can you take it down?” she asked.

There was a pause.

“Do you want me to?”

Sophie looked at her own profile.

Ryan appeared everywhere.

His hand.

His shoulder.

His flowers.

His apartment.

His shadow.

Enough evidence to prove she was loved.

Not enough to prove by whom.

“Yes,” she said.

Ryan removed the photograph.

Sophie thanked him.

That evening, she posted another picture of the flowers.

They had begun to wilt.

The angle was kinder.

Some things are better kept private.

Ryan did not like this one.
