FILTHY RICH

Season Two — The Relationship

Episode 04 — Shared Location

Grace and Ethan had been together for six months.

Their phones had been together for three.

One Tuesday morning, Grace received a notification.

Ethan has arrived at work.

She hadn’t asked.

She had simply forgotten to turn the feature off after a weekend trip.

It felt harmless.

Until it became information.

At lunch, Ethan stopped replying for forty-three minutes.

Grace noticed.

Not because she was waiting.

Because his location hadn’t moved.

She opened the map.

Still at the office.

She closed it.

Then opened it again.

At 2:17 p.m., the blue dot moved across the street.

Coffee.

Probably.

At 2:19, it stopped.

Grace zoomed in.

The café had outdoor seating.

She imagined sunlight.

A woman from accounting.

Laughter.

She closed the app.

Then opened Instagram.

Nothing.

LinkedIn.

Nothing.

The map remained the only person talking.

That evening, Ethan arrived home carrying two coffees.

“I thought you’d still be working.”

“I finished early.”

Grace nodded.

“I saw.”

Ethan looked at her.

“Saw what?”

“You went to Harper’s Café.”

“I did.”

“Nice.”

“It was.”

There was a pause.

“Were you there too?”

Grace laughed.

“No.”

“Then how do you know?”

She looked at the coffees.

“You shared your location.”

“Oh.”

He smiled.

“I forgot.”

“So did I.”

Neither of them believed her.

The following Saturday, Ethan spent the afternoon buying a birthday present for his sister.

He visited four shops.

Grace watched none of them.

Until the fourth.

The map showed him inside a jewellery store.

She frowned.

Then smiled.

Then frowned again.

When Ethan came home, he looked unusually pleased with himself.

“How was your day?” Grace asked.

“Productive.”

“What did you buy?”

“A gift.”

“For who?”

“My sister.”

Grace nodded too quickly.

That night, she lay awake.

Not because she thought he was lying.

Because she had already imagined two different futures before he unlocked the front door.

On Monday, Ethan sent her flowers.

No occasion.

Just flowers.

Grace smiled.

Then checked his location.

He was at work.

The flowers had been ordered online.

She wondered whether that counted.

The next weekend, Ethan found Grace staring at her phone.

“What are you looking at?”

“You.”

He laughed.

“I’ve been on the sofa for twenty minutes.”

“I know.”

She locked the screen.

“I think we should turn this off.”

“The location?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Grace searched for the right answer.

Because she trusted him.

Because she didn’t trust herself.

Because information had become imagination.

Because blue dots were terrible storytellers.

“I miss wondering,” she said.

Ethan picked up his phone.

He switched off location sharing.

Then handed it to her.

Grace did the same.

For a moment, nothing happened.

The world became strangely quiet.

That evening, Ethan walked to the supermarket.

Grace did not know when he left.

She did not know which route he took.

She tried to read.

Forty-seven minutes later, he returned carrying ice cream.

“Long queue?” she asked.

“Not really.”

Grace looked at the bag.

She almost asked where else he had been.

That night, she opened the map.

Location unavailable.

Grace refreshed it.
